# Mars Climate Orbiter (1998-1999): one wrong unit

## Verified facts
- Launched Dec. 11, 1998; lost on Mars arrival Sept. 23, 1999, after a 9-month trip. (MIB Phase I Report: https://llis.nasa.gov/llis_lib/pdf/1009464main1_0641-mr.pdf)
- Root cause: thruster data ("Small Forces"/AMD file) was delivered in pound-force seconds; the spec and navigation software expected newton-seconds. 1 lbf = 4.45 N, so the effect was low by a factor of 4.45. (same report)
- Planned first closest approach 226 km; minimum survivable altitude 80 km; after-the-fact estimate 57 km, judged too low for survival. (same report)
- Carrier signal last seen 09:04:52 UTC Sept. 23, 1999, 49 s earlier than predicted. (same report)
- NASA Science summarizes: English vs metric error; the craft burned up in Mars' atmosphere: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-climate-orbiter/
- Not used: cost figures (not verified in a source I opened). No dialogue or quotes.

## Shots (~37 s, 9:16)
1. **hook 0-3 s** "One wrong unit lost a spaceship." Gold orbiter tumbles toward the camera over a big Mars; label "LB ≠ N". Push-in. Violet/black night, red Mars.
2. **launch 3-9 s** Rocket lifts off a green pad with flash, smoke burst and shake; label "9 MONTHS TO MARS"; badge 1998 / Dec. 11. Camera tilts up with the rocket. Sky blue to peach.
3. **mixup 9-18 s** Coral GROUND box (POUNDS) and sky CRAFT box (NEWTONS); a yellow data pulse crosses, turns coral, flash + shake on arrival, labels "x4.45" and "1 lbf = 4.45 N". Slow push-in. Dark navy.
4. **approach 18-26 s** Orbiter over Mars limb; green PLAN 226 KM arc, yellow SAFE 80 KM+ line, coral REAL 57 KM path; craft dips onto the real path and starts to glow. Stylized, not to scale. Deep purple.
5. **loss 26-32 s** Craft streaks into the atmosphere, heats, white flash + burst + shake at the key moment; "SIGNAL LOST", "49 SEC EARLY"; badge 1999 / Sept. 23. Maroon.
6. **payoff 32-37 s** LB and N boxes slide together, gold ring, burst; "CHECK YOUR UNITS", "1 lbf = 4.45 N". Teal. Pays off the hook.
