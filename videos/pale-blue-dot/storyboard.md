# Pale Blue Dot (Voyager 1, Feb. 14, 1990)

## Verified facts (opened myself)
- Photo taken Feb. 14, 1990 by Voyager 1, 3.7 billion miles (6 billion km) from the Sun; inspired the title of Carl Sagan's book *Pale Blue Dot: A Vision of the Human Future in Space*. https://science.nasa.gov/resource/voyager-1s-pale-blue-dot/
- Part of the first-ever "family portrait" of the solar system; 60 frames acquired; Earth was a crescent only 0.12 pixels in size; Earth lies in the center of a scattered light ray caused by shooting so close to the Sun. https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA00452
- Sagan quote printed by NASA: "Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us." (same pages)

## Shots (artist's reenactment, no invented dialogue)
1. hook 0-3 s - "That tiny dot is all of us." Voyager in foreground, glowing blue dot far behind, slow push toward the dot. Flash at 0.1 s. Deep indigo/black.
2. distance 3-9 s - "On February 14, 1990, Voyager 1 was about six billion kilometers from the Sun." Probe drifts right; small sun left; row of dots + label 6 BILLION KM. Camera pan. Warm sun vs cold indigo.
3. frames 9-15 s - "It took sixty frames, the first family portrait of the solar system." 60 tiles pop in (10x6), flash at completion. Camera pull-back. Teal/violet.
4. pixel 15-22 s - "Earth fills less than one pixel: a crescent 0.12 of a pixel wide." One big cream pixel, a thin blue sliver (0.12 wide) inside. Camera push-in. Label 1 PIXEL / 0.12.
5. ray 22-26 s - "A beam of scattered sunlight runs right through it." Vertical light ray, blue dot at center, push in.
6. payoff 26-34 s - "Carl Sagan named his book after it. Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us." Pale blue dot, camera pulls back, waving figures below; flash on "us".
