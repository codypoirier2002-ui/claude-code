# Galileo spots Jupiter's moons (1610)

## Verified facts (NASA, https://www.nasa.gov/general/415-years-ago-astronomer-galileo-discovers-jupiters-moons/)
- Jan. 7, 1610: Galileo used his newly improved, homemade 20-power telescope on Jupiter.
- He saw three points of light near it and at first took them for distant stars.
- Over several nights they moved relative to each other but stayed near Jupiter.
- Four days later he saw a fourth point of light behaving the same way.
- By Jan. 15 he concluded he had found four moons orbiting Jupiter: strong evidence for the Copernican theory.
- Second source: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/galileos-observations-of-the-moon-jupiter-venus-and-the-sun/

2. telescope (3-11 s) Galileo figure + homemade scope, small Jupiter in sky. Label 20x. Camera drifts up. Night blue/green.
3. three-lights (11-16 s) Close on Jupiter, three pulsing dots labeled "STAR?". Push-in.
4. shifting (16-21 s) NIGHT 1/2/3, dots change places, always beside Jupiter. Static-ish camera.
5. fourth (21-26 s) "4 days later", a mint fourth dot pops with flash and burst. Purple mood.
6. four-moons (26-35 s) Four moons orbit Jupiter, flash, "NOT EVERYTHING CIRCLES EARTH". Camera pulls back; payoff of hook.
