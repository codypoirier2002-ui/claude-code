# The bridge that danced itself apart (1940)

Artist's reenactment: stylized 3D, not to scale. Nothing invented: no dialogue, people or vehicles shown.

## Verified facts (sources opened)
- Opened July 1, 1940; third longest suspension span in the world; collapsed in a windstorm Nov. 7, 1940, "only four months later"; nickname "Galloping Gertie". WSDOT: https://www.wsdot.wa.gov/tnbhistory/
- Primary explanation is "torsional flutter": a self-induced vibration; once the deck twisted, its own motion produced the forces; the deck was narrow and shallow (depth-to-width 1 to 72), so very flexible. The exact cause is still debated by experts. WSDOT: https://wsdot.wa.gov/tnbhistory/bridges-failure.htm
- Since 1940, designs are "aerodynamically streamlined, or stiffened against torsional motion, or both" (same WSDOT page).
- The deck moved up and down noticeably in moderate wind during construction and after opening; the common "resonance" explanation was rejected by the Federal Works Administration investigation as "improbable". APS News: https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2016/11/collapse-of-tacoma-narrows-bridge ; Billah & Scanlan, Am. J. Phys. 59 (1991) 118-124.
