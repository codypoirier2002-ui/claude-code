# Pigeons, a hiss, and the Big Bang's echo (1960s)

## Verified facts
- Bell Labs built a 20-foot horn antenna in Holmdel, NJ in 1960 for the Echo satellite system; Telstar made it obsolete, so Penzias and Wilson used it as a radio telescope. (APS: https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2002/07/discovery-cosmic-microwave-background)
- They found a uniform microwave noise (7.35 cm wavelength) from all directions; they trapped pigeons nesting in the horn and spent hours removing droppings. (APS, same page)
- After about a year of ruling out causes, including pigeons nesting in the antenna, they concluded the noise was microwaves throughout the universe at about three degrees above absolute zero. Robert Dicke at Princeton supplied the explanation: the Big Bang's faint cold afterglow. (Nobel speed read: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1978/speedread/)
- Penzias and Wilson received the 1978 Nobel Prize in Physics for discovering the cosmic microwave background. (Nobel, APS)
- Discovery year differs across sources, so the video says "the 1960s".
